\thesisinstitution{Duke University}

% "All pages in the thesis/dissertation must be formatted with a 1.5-inch margin
% on the left side and a 1-inch margin on the top, right side, and bottom." The
% guide adds that "the 1-inch margin at the bottom is measured from below the
% page number", and that all text -- footnotes, page numbers, tables, figures,
% schemes and legends -- must fall within these margins.
%
% Duke is one of only three schools in this set with an asymmetric margin (with
% Georgia Tech and, conditionally, Yale), and it is unconditional here: the
% wider left margin is not contingent on ordering a bound copy.
\thesissetgeometry{left=1.5in, right=1in, top=1in, bottom=1in}

% "All text (including the abstract) must be double-spaced", with single spacing
% within multi-line entries in the table of contents and the lists, within
% references, in chapter headings and subheadings longer than one line, in
% indented quotations, and in footnotes and figure/table captions.
\thesissetspacing{double}

% --- Accessibility -----------------------------------------------------------
% Duke's "Document Accessibility" section is the outlier of this whole set: it
% requires nothing. Quoted in full because paraphrasing it would round it toward
% the obligation every other school in the top twenty is moving to, and because
% the guide is from 2020 -- this is very likely the entry that changes first.
%
% \thesisrequiretagging is NOT set. This class still emits a tagged PDF, which
% goes beyond what Duke asks; that is the library's default, not Duke's rule.
\thesisaccessibilityrequirement{Discretionary}
	{"If you are interested in making your thesis or dissertation an accessible
	 document for the vision-impaired, we suggest these valuable resources to
	 use at your own discretion" -- Duke's web accessibility service and
	 section508.gov. Accessibility is offered, not required, in the 2020 guide.}
\thesisaccessibilityrequirement{Typeface}
	{"All text must be in a commonly accepted font in your field (typically
	 Times Roman, Garamond, Arial, or Helvetica) and consistently used
	 throughout the document." Colour and underlining are permitted only in
	 figures, tables, or examples of coding.}
